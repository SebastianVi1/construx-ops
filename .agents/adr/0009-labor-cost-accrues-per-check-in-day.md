# Labor cost accrues per Check-in day at the rate effective that day, computed and never retroactive

Status: proposed (2026-11-15)

A Site's Accrued labor cost is one Labor rate for every day a person has a non-removed Check-in on that Site. The rate is the one in force for that person on that Site at the end of that day: the Real wage its Labor category had that day when the rate follows the Catalog, or the fixed negotiated amount when it does not. Cost history is non-retroactive: a new Cost, a FASAR recalculation or a new Labor rate applies from the day it is recorded onward and never changes an earlier day. The accrual is computed on every read from Check-ins, Labor rates and Cost history; no Expense rows are written for it. The Site's budget status counts it beside recorded Expenses, and corrections (a bonus, a missed day) are ordinary Expenses of category `LABOR`.

Why: Check-ins are the only record of who worked where (ADR 0007), so they are the only defensible base for labor cost; a calendar-day accrual would charge days nobody came. Writing an Expense per person per day would create thousands of rows that must be deleted or re-priced whenever a Check-in is removed or a wage changes, and would blur the line between what the company recorded spending and what the system inferred. Non-retroactive history keeps every past figure stable: a cost report printed yesterday still reconciles today, and a wage update does not silently reprice last month. Fixed negotiated rates exist because real wages are agreed per person on some Sites; letting them follow the Catalog would reprice a handshake.

Considered and rejected: one Expense row per person per day (materialized, fragile under Check-in removal and wage changes, mixes inferred and recorded spending); calendar-day accrual from the assignment's dates (charges absences, contradicts ADR 0007); manual-only labor expenses (the current state, which is why labor is under-recorded on every Site); retroactive repricing when a cost or rate changes (unstable past figures, needs an approval trail).

Consequences: Accrued labor cost is not payroll and must not be presented as what was paid; it is a cost estimate for budget control. Removing a Check-in the same day removes that day's accrual, which is intended. A Check-in without a Labor rate counts a day at zero and is flagged, so missing rates are visible rather than hidden. Cost history entries cannot be deleted or backdated, because the accrual of a past day depends on them; a mistake is fixed by a new entry. Hours, overtime, and automatic equipment accrual are not covered and would need their own records (and ADRs).
